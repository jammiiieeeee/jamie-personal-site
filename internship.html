An internship on the factory floor

E-Learning Development · Articulate 360 · Manufacturing Transformation

I spent my internship at Johnson Electric — a global precision manufacturing company with 35,000+ employees across 20 countries, producing motors, solenoids, and motion subsystems for automotive, industrial, and medical applications. Founded in 1959, listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange since 1984, and holding the #1 market share in automotive micro-motors.

My mandate was simple in words and complex in execution: build e-learning modules that actually train operators — not compliance checkboxes, not PDFs dressed up as courses. Real training, for real production lines, where the person taking the course is the person who will stand in front of the machine tomorrow. The standard you meet is the standard they carry into the factory.

What I did

Four responsibilities, one goal: turn paper training into something that scales.

  • 01

    Content Development

    Built interactive e-learning modules using Articulate Storyline 360, tailored to manufacturing workflows and operator training needs. Each module mapped directly to a real station on a real production line.

  • 02

    Instructional Design

    Applied adult learning principles to structure content — from storyboarding through final production. Every screen had to earn its place. If it didn't help someone on the line do their job better, it didn't survive review.

  • 03

    Knowledge Checks & Assessment

    Developed quizzes and scenario-based assessments that verify comprehension — not the "what year was this founded" kind, but "what do you do when the pressure gauge reads red" kind. The kind that matters when the machine is running.

  • 04

    LMS Deployment

    Packaged SCORM-compliant modules and deployed to the corporate Learning Management System for global access. One upload, available to every site, every shift.

The shift was real. Before: instructor-led sessions with printed manuals — one trainer, one location, one shift at a time. After: SCORM-compliant modules accessible 24/7, standardized globally, with complete LMS analytics and completion tracking. The training infrastructure caught up to the production infrastructure.

What I learned

Three things I now understand about the intersection of manufacturing and education.

  • 01

    Content is infrastructure

    Well-designed training modules are as critical to production lines as the machinery itself. A machine without a trained operator is just a machine. A trained operator without a maintained curriculum is a trained operator waiting to forget. Content, done right, is not a nice-to-have. It's part of the production line.

  • 02

    Learning must match the line speed

    As production lines evolve from manual to semi to full automation, training must keep pace. You can't teach automated equipment with a printed binder. Digital learning scales with the business — it doesn't have to wait for the right trainer to be available on the right shift at the right site.

  • 03

    Data-driven learning works

    LMS analytics close the feedback loop that classroom training leaves open. Track completions. Measure retention. Prove ROI. When you can show a factory manager that the operators who took the module have 40% fewer quality defects, the conversation changes from "should we do e-learning" to "when can we get the next module."
