Dear you,

Here's what's happening.

I'm in a season of building things that earn their keep. Not things that look good in a portfolio — things that solve actual problems for actual people. The kind of work where the win isn't a launch post; it's someone saying "this just works" and meaning it. That's been my bar and it's a harder bar than it sounds. Getting there means saying no to cleverness more often than I'd like. The cleverness is fun. The usefulness is the job.

I've been thinking a lot about craft — not as a performance, not as perfectionism dressed up in a nicer word, but as attention paid at every level. The person who uses what you built doesn't care about your architecture. They care that it works. They care that it doesn't frustrate them. They care that someone thought about them while making it. Everything else is a conversation between engineers, and that conversation has its place, but it's not the product. I've been trying to stay closer to the product.

On my desk: a notebook I've been carrying for three years, still only half-full. A mechanical pencil I stole from somewhere in 2019 that I will never replace. A stack of books I'm "currently reading" — the quotes are doing a lot of work there. The desk is a museum of intentions. I'm okay with that. The half-full notebook means I'm still thinking. The pencil means I'm sentimental about the right things. The stack means I keep reaching for more than I can hold. These are all good signs.

I'm reading The Creative Act by Rick Rubin and finding it more useful than a book about creativity has any right to be. It's not about making art — it's about noticing what's already there and getting out of its way. I keep underlining sentences and writing "yes" in the margin. That's either a sign that the book is brilliant or that I'm easy to impress. I'm choosing to believe the first one.

What I care about, right now: things that last longer than the tool that made them. Code that someone will still run in five years. Interfaces that feel inevitable, not trendy. The people who taught me to care about any of this — I think about them more than they know. I'm trying to be one of those people for someone else. That's the long game.

What I'm done with: pretending to care about things that don't matter. Hype cycles. Tools that solve problems I don't have. The phrase "move fast and break things" and anyone who still says it without irony. I'm done with building for the approval of people I wouldn't ask for advice. That one took a while.

This page changes when something fundamental shifts — not when I ship a feature, but when I notice the ground has moved. Check back when the seasons change. I'll be here, probably with a different book and the same pencil.

Talk soon,

Jamie

Typed in one sitting. Kept honest by the voice in my head.
