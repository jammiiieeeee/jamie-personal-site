The deal

No fluff. I'll tell you what I actually believe.

I try to build things that are useful. I try to make the people around me better at what they do. And I don't ignore the voice in my head that says "this isn't right yet"That voice is annoying. It's also usually correct. Don't tell it I said that.. I listen to that voice. I build what it asks for. Sometimes it takes weeks. Sometimes it takes one more try at 2am when you should've gone to bed.

I chase flow state — the thing that happens when you've practiced something so deeply that your hands know what to do before your brain does. On a piano, that's muscle memory. Your fingers find the keys and you're just in the music, not performing it. In code, it's when the architecture finally snaps into place and you stop fighting the tool and start making. Both feel the same. Both are the reason I do this.

I give a damn. Embarrassingly so. It's my most expensive personality trait and also the only one I'd keep.

Things I've shipped

  • 2025–2026

    Score Extractor

    Python · PyQt6 · OpenCV · PaddleOCR

    A desktop app that turns a video of page-turning sheet music into a clean, printable PDF. Automatic page-turn detection, hand-overlay removal, and self-tuning computer vision pipeline. Built for music teachers and pianists.

    Real talk: 374 tests. Clean Architecture with dependency injection. An adaptive calibration system that learns the threshold for bar detection per video so it never guesses wrong. Custom dark theme with brass accents. PyInstaller onefile .exe. I built a self-tuning CV pipeline in a desktop app with a custom design system. I don't know what "over-engineering" means and I refuse to learn.

  • 2025

    2D Factory Simulation

    TypeScript · Phaser · Supabase

    A production-line training simulator for factory workers — 17-station motor assembly, real-time decision-making, and a full FSM-driven engine. Built for actual manufacturing floors.

    Real talk: I taught a WebGL game engine to behave like an industrial training tool. 17 stations, 12 entity types, an event bus with 3 architectural layers. This thing has more state machines than some startups.

  • 2024

    AeroSky

    Three.js · Vanilla JS · Zero dependencies

    Ten interactive physics simulations in the browser — orbital mechanics, wave propagation, quantum foam, fluid surfaces. Each one a real physical model you can manipulate directly.

    Real talk: 4,000 lines of vanilla JS, zero build step, runs straight from index.html. I built a code-gen pipeline that writes new simulations automatically because I got tired of doing it by hand.

  • 2024

    PPT TTS

    Python · AWS Polly · Tkinter

    A desktop app that adds neural text-to-speech narration to training presentations. Drag in a PPT, get back fully-narrated slides ready for e-learning import.

    Real talk: 1,300-line Tkinter GUI with animated breathe effects on the Generate button. The button literally pulses. I built a pronunciation correction system because AWS Polly can't say "stator."
